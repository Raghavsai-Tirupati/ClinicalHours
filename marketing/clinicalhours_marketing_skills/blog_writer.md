# Skill: blog_writer

## Purpose
Write SEO-targeted blog posts that attract pre-med students searching for practical clinical hours guidance. Every post should rank for a real search query AND convert readers to sign up.

## Inputs Needed
- Target keyword or topic
- Funnel stage: awareness (broad) or consideration (specific pre-med process)
- Desired length: short (600–800 words) or long (1,200–1,800 words)
- Any specific angle or hook to lead with

## What This Skill Produces
- Full blog post draft with H1, H2s, intro, body, CTA
- Meta title and meta description (for SEO)
- Internal link suggestions (where to link to ClinicalHours features)
- One suggested social pull-quote from the post

---

## Content Strategy

### High-Value Keyword Categories

**Informational / Top of Funnel**
- "how many clinical hours do you need for medical school"
- "what counts as clinical experience for med school"
- "how to find clinical volunteer opportunities as a pre-med"
- "difference between clinical and non-clinical hours AMCAS"

**Process / Mid Funnel**
- "how to log clinical hours for AMCAS"
- "how to write AMCAS clinical experience description"
- "AAMC core competencies pre-med checklist"
- "how to track volunteer hours for medical school application"

**Local / Programmatic**
- "clinical volunteer opportunities [city]"
- "free health clinic volunteer [city]"
- "pre-med volunteer programs [university name]"

## Post Structure

### Standard Format
1. **H1 (target keyword-aligned):** Direct and specific
2. **Intro (2–3 sentences):** State the problem or question, promise the answer. No fluff.
3. **Body H2s:** Each section answers one sub-question. Use short paragraphs.
4. **CTA block:** Natural plug for ClinicalHours — not forced. "If you want to track all of this in one place, ClinicalHours is free to sign up."
5. **Closing:** One sentence wrap-up. No "in conclusion."

### Tone Rules (from brand_voice.md)
- Write like a knowledgeable friend, not a textbook
- Short sentences, active voice
- No corporate language
- Specific numbers and facts wherever possible (cite AAMC, AMCAS documentation)
- Humor is fine if it's dry and relatable — never forced

### What to Avoid
- Fluff intros ("In today's competitive medical school landscape...")
- Vague advice ("Make sure to track your hours consistently")
- Overselling ClinicalHours mid-post — one CTA at the end is enough
- Listicles with no substance — each point needs real explanation
