# Skill: student_positioning

## Purpose
Define and sharpen the messaging angle for reaching pre-med students. Use this before writing any student-facing copy, ads, or content.

## Inputs Needed
- Current page or copy you want to position against (optional)
- Specific funnel stage: awareness, consideration, or conversion
- Channel: Instagram, LinkedIn, email, landing page, blog

## What This Skill Produces
1. ICP snapshot (who exactly we're talking to)
2. Top 3 positioning angles with rationale
3. Key message for each angle (one sentence each)
4. What to lead with vs. what to save for later
5. Phrases and framings to avoid

---

## ICP: Pre-Med Student

**Demographics**
- Undergraduate freshman through senior (ages 18–22), plus post-bacc/gap year
- Enrolled at a 4-year university
- Planning to apply to medical school within 1–4 years

**Psychographics**
- Hyper-aware of their application competitiveness
- Always feels behind on hours, research, shadowing, volunteering
- Skeptical of anything that wastes their time — they've been burned before
- Heavily influenced by peers, pre-med advisors, Reddit (r/premed), Discord servers
- Motivated by concrete outcomes, not inspiration

**Core Jobs-to-be-Done**
1. Find clinical opportunities near campus or home
2. Log hours in a way that transfers directly to AMCAS
3. Write the activity description without making it sound bad
4. Know their competitive standing (hours, competencies) before applying

**Where They Hang Out**
- r/premed, r/medicalschool
- Pre-med Discord servers
- GroupMe/group chats by major or pre-med club
- Instagram (passive scrolling)
- LinkedIn (mainly for internships, not community)
- Pre-med club listservs and emails

## Positioning Angles

### Angle 1: The Logistics Problem
**Frame:** "Finding and tracking clinical hours is more annoying than it should be."
**Message:** "ClinicalHours puts the opportunities and the tracker in one place so you stop losing hours."
**Best for:** Awareness, top of funnel, social content
**Lead with:** Frustration/relatability
**Don't:** Pitch premium features here — just get them to understand the free value first

### Angle 2: The AMCAS Stakes
**Frame:** "You'll need 150–200+ hours documented the right way when you apply."
**Message:** "Most students don't realize their logs aren't AMCAS-ready until it's too late."
**Best for:** Consideration, email, landing page
**Lead with:** Specificity — cite the hour range, cite AMCAS
**Don't:** Be fear-mongering — keep it matter-of-fact

### Angle 3: The Application Edge
**Frame:** "The activity description and competency tracker are where you actually differentiate."
**Message:** "ClinicalHours Premium helps you write the AMCAS description and track AAMC competencies — the two things that turn hours into an application."
**Best for:** Conversion, premium upsell, email to existing free users
**Lead with:** Outcome (a better application), not features
**Don't:** Use AI jargon — just say it helps you write it

---

## Phrases to Avoid
- "Join our community" — they don't care about community, they care about outcomes
- "Streamline your journey" — vague and corporate
- "Empowering pre-med students" — sounds like every other app
- Anything that implies the app does the work for them (they're skeptical of AI doing med school stuff)
